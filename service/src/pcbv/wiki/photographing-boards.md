# Photographing boards

Your photos are the map for the rest of the work. You mark headers on them and read chip markings from them. You can also compare them with photos that other people have posted. A few habits make the photos much more useful.

## Light

- Use even, soft light: daylight from a window, or two lamps from opposite sides.
- Do not use the flash. It makes glare on the chips and on the solder.
- To read laser-etched markings on a chip, shine a phone torch across it at a low angle.

## Camera

- Hold the camera straight above the board, so the image is not skewed. Fill the frame.
- Tap to focus on the board and hold still. Take a few photos and keep the sharpest.
- A phone camera is good enough. Move closer instead of using the digital zoom.

## What to shoot

- Take the whole front and the whole back. Add them as **front** and **back**.
- Take close-ups of the main chips and of anything that looks like a header. Add them as **detail**.
- Take the label on the case, for the model and the revision.

The app keeps your original photo on this machine. It puts a smaller copy without location data into git.
